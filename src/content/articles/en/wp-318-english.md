<p>Visitors to the home of Tuti, aged 36, in Temiang Village find a variety of rhizome crops growing around her house. These plants are used both as cooking ingredients and in traditional herbal preparations.</p>
<p>Continuing knowledge passed down through generations, Tuti makes jamu from rhizomes and other plants for residents of Temiang, Bandar Laksamana District, Bengkalis Regency.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Persiapan-Pembuatan-Jamu.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Preparing ingredients for jamu</em></p>
<p>“We still make postnatal jamu when people order it. Customers come not only from Temiang but also from neighbouring villages,” Tuti told Yayasan Gambut by telephone on Wednesday, 25/11/2020.</p>
<p>The article describes a local belief that postnatal jamu supports recovery and circulation after childbirth. Within this tradition, it is consumed during the 44 days following birth.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Penjemuran-Obat.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Drying jamu ingredients</em></p>
<p><strong>Temiang’s Jamu Package</strong></p>
<p>The package contains 2 preparations: 1 for drinking and 1 for applying to the face and body, traditionally intended to prevent what is locally called masuk angin. It was sold for Rp 280,000, with each preparation weighing 1/2 kg and a reported storage life of 4 months after preparation.</p>
<p>Tuti described the ingredients as follows:</p>
<p>For the drink: turmeric, red ginger, kencur, a rempah ratus herbal mixture, coriander, black pepper, cinnamon bark, star anise, cloves, nutmeg, buah telaga, cumin, lada manis and air lebur. The ingredients are dried and then pounded.</p>
<p>For the body powder: rempah param, turmeric, bongle and kencur, dried and pounded together with rice flour.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/jamu-jadi-1.png" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Finely ground jamu for drinking and for use as body powder</em></p>
<p>“We plan to grow red ginger with a group of 11 women. We have already collected 1000 growing sacks ourselves and hope Yayasan Gambut can support us with planting material,” Tuti said.</p>
