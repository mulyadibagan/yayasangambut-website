<p>Penampi is one of the villages supplying pineapples to Bengkalis Island and surrounding areas. At the time of reporting, pineapple cultivation covered 61 hectares in this predominantly peatland village in Bengkalis District.</p>
<p>Samrizal, aged 31, explained that not all pineapples command high market prices. Processing can increase their economic value and shelf life. Through the village-owned enterprise he manages, residents have developed pineapple dodol, syrup and crackers.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Nenas-2-1024x460.png" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Penampi pineapple products: dodol, syrup and crackers</em></p>
<p><strong>Pineapple Dodol</strong></p>
<p>Samrizal uses small pineapples that sell cheaply in the fresh market. He can produce 25 kg of dodol monthly, priced at Rp 69,000 per 1 kg or Rp 35,000 per half-kilogram. The packaged product is reported to keep for up to 2 months.</p>
<p><strong>Pineapple Syrup</strong></p>
<p>Syrup is packaged in 250 ml bottles and sold for Rp 13,000 per bottle.</p>
<p><strong>Pineapple Crackers</strong></p>
<p>Ready-to-eat pineapple crackers are sold in packets for Rp 10,000 each.</p>
<p>The products are marketed online and through the Bengkalis Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KADIN) stand.</p>
<p>“We hope consumers will choose Penampi’s pineapple products as distinctive gifts from Bengkalis, and that government and other partners will help market products from this crop, which grows well on peatland,” Samrizal told Yayasan Gambut on Monday, 23/11/20.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/nenas-3-1-1024x376.png" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Left: BRG field school visit. Right: Bengkalis Polytechnic visit.</em></p>
<p>The initiative has attracted visits from several organisations, including the Peatland Restoration Agency’s field school and Bengkalis State Polytechnic.</p>
