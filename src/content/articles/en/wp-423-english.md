<p>The coronavirus disease 2019 (Covid-19) pandemic affected social conditions, economic activity and community welfare. The resulting slowdown threatened the national economy and created a need for recovery measures.</p>
<p>One government strategy to support community economic recovery was the 2020 labour-intensive mangrove planting programme in Panipahan Laut, Pasir Limau Kapas District, Rokan Hilir Regency, Riau Province.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Kawasan-Degradasi-mangrove-1024x480.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Area designated for mangrove planting in Panipahan Laut. Photo: KPH Bagan Siapi-Api, 2020</em></p>
<p>According to Arifin, Head of the Bagan Siapi-Api Forest Management Unit (KPH), degraded mangroves covered 250 hectares. The community-managed labour programme planted 25 hectares, providing income for residents involved in the work.</p>
<p>The site also lies within the working area of BPDASHL Indragiri Rokan. The programme planted 82,500 api-api (Avicennia) seedlings and provided 1,520 person-days of work for Panipahan Laut residents.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/penanaman-mangrove-1024x480.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Community mangrove planting. Photo: KPH Bagan Siapi-Api, 2020</em></p>
<p>“The 2020 labour-intensive mangrove planting activity was a government policy response through the Ministry of Environment and Forestry’s National Economic Recovery Programme,” Arifin told Yayasan Gambut on Monday, 11/01/2021.</p>
