<p>Rudiyanto, aged 40, is a farmer in Penampi Village, Bengkalis District. In 2016, he initially cleared land for pineapple by burning, at a time when outreach on the effects of peatland fires was limited. After attending a Peatland Restoration Agency (BRG) field school in Siak Regency in 2019, he began learning more about farming without burning.</p>
<p>He first grew bitter melon around his home on deep peat, then watermelon and other crops. Initially working independently, he was invited to share his experience with other villages. A barrier for others interested in adopting the approach was the capital needed to begin planting.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pelatihan-F1-Embrio.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Rudiyanto providing training in making F1 Mbio</em></p>
<p>Yayasan Gambut wanted to help share his enthusiasm with others committed to farming without burning and to promote sustainably grown peatland produce. Rudiyanto subsequently formed a group of 4 people with support from the foundation.</p>
<p><strong>Using F1 Mbio</strong></p>
<p>Before planting, Rudiyanto and his colleagues apply a preparation they call F1 Mbio. Made from materials readily available on Bengkalis Island, it is intended to accelerate organic matter decomposition and reduce soil acidity.</p>
<p>“Prepare 5 litres of water, 1 kg of rice bran, 1 kg of sugar, 1 pineapple, 1/2 kg of shrimp and around 2 ons (Indonesian weight units) of sago flour. Cook the ingredients, let them cool, then add about 1 spoonful of free-range chicken manure collected before sunrise. Ferment for 1 day and 1 night,” Rudiyanto explained to Yayasan Gambut by telephone on Tuesday, 24/11/2020.</p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/F1-Mbio-1024x768.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Boiling ingredients for F1 Mbio. Photo: Rudiyanto</em></p>
<p>For 1 hectare, he mixes 5 litres of F1 Embrio with 200 litres of water and applies it to the peatland 1 week before planting. He said it can also be mixed with approximately 50 kg of animal manure.</p>
<p><strong>Managing Fire Danger Information</strong></p>
<p>Alongside farming, Rudiyanto maintains a forest and land fire danger information board to raise community awareness of risk. He manually changes its colour indicators using information from BMKG’s <a href="https://www.bmkg.go.id/cuaca/kebakaran-hutan.bmkg?index=dc&amp;wil=riau&amp;day=obs">Forest and Land Fire Warning System (SPARTAN)</a>.</p>

<p><strong>Harvesting Chilli on Peatland Without Burning</strong></p>
<p><img src="https://www.yayasangambut.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Cabe-Penampi-2-1024x768.jpeg" alt=""></p>
<p><em>Bird’s-eye chilli grown on Rudiyanto’s peatland plot</em></p>
<p>At the time of reporting, Rudiyanto had harvested 40 kg of bird’s-eye chilli from 300 plants, selling it for Rp 30,000 per kg. Produce was sold to nearby residents and supplied to Pasar Memanah in Bengkalis town.</p>
